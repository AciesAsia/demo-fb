Italian and Asian cuisines account for 60% of Q1 revenue ($97,040), driven by price premium and order volume respectively. Dinner service is the primary revenue engine across all seven days. Monday leads all days at $2,017 average daily revenue. Eleven underperforming menu items should be removed to simplify the kitchen and concentrate revenue on proven performers. By order volume, Hamburger is the most-ordered item overall (629 orders), followed by Edamame (625) and Korean Beef Bowl (592) - material for stock planning and kitchen throughput.
Key Takeaways
Nine findings ranked by operational significance.

Italian: Price-Led Dominance

Italian generates the most revenue ($49,851) at the highest average price ($16.78/item) despite having fewer items sold than Asian. Price is the driver, not volume.

Korean Beef Bowl — Anchor Item

Single highest-earning item at $10,626 accounting for 6.6% of Q1 revenue alone.
Must be retained and actively promoted at every service.

Volume vs Revenue: Hamburger vs Edamame

Hamburger leads by count (629 orders).
Edamame ranks 2nd by volume (625 orders) but generates only $3,125 due to its $5.00 price point— a high-frequency, low-value item.

Monday Dinner Is a Footfall Story

Monday's $477 revenue advantage over Wednesday is 69.8% explained by dinner volume: 469 covers vs 337. Average spend per dinner order is nearly identical ($28.51 vs $26.98). The gap is who comes in, not how much they spend.

February: Shorter Month Effect

February achieves the best average daily revenue ($1,832) because 28 days concentrates strong weekday evenings into fewer days — not a demand spike.

Holidays: Dinner-Only Operation

Holiday dinner revenue per table ($67.74) is the highest of any segment. Holidays average 17% above regular days. Recommended: dinner-only service 17:00–22:00 on major holidays.

11 Items for Removal

Review-quadrant items contribute $31,942 (19.9% of revenue) but generate disproportionate kitchen complexity. Chicken Tacos (1.4 orders/day) is the weakest item and is recommended for immediate removal.

Wednesday 16:00 Anomaly

Wednesday 16:00 generates 80 orders and $2,484 - the highest 16:00 of any day. The gap vs Monday is in the 19:00–22:00 window. Targeted evening incentives will be more effective than a generic Wednesday promotion.

Italian — Price-Led Performance: Top revenue category at $49,851.50 (31.0%). Achieves this with only 2,971 items sold because its average item price of $16.78 is the highest of any cuisine. Spaghetti & Meatballs ($8,508) and Eggplant Parmesan ($7,153) are the two strongest performers. Items priced at $14.50 or above consistently generate above-average revenue. Fettuccine Alfredo (250 orders) and Cheese Lasagna (210 orders) fall into the Review quadrant and are candidates for removal.
Asian — Volume-Led Performance: Generates $47,188.30 (29.3%) through the highest item volume (3,505 items sold). Korean Beef Bowl ($10,626, 592 orders) and Tofu Pad Thai ($8,251, 569 orders) anchor the category. Edamame (625 orders, $5.00) is the second-most-ordered item in the dataset — ranked behind Hamburger (629 orders) — and generates only $3,125 in revenue. It is a high-frequency side item, not a revenue driver. Potstickers (207 orders) and California Roll (362 orders) fall below the Review threshold.
Mexican — Mid-Tier with Concentration Risk: Generates $35,181.10 (21.9%). Steak Torta (491 orders, $6,849) and Chicken Burrito (459 orders, $5,944) carry the category. Six of nine Mexican items fall into Review or Workhorse quadrants. Chicken Tacos is the worst-performing item in the entire menu with 124 orders at $11.95 over 90 days — equating to 1.4 orders per day. Recommended for immediate removal.
American — Price-Ceiling Constraint: Generates the least revenue ($28,561.65, 17.8%) despite competitive order volumes. The ceiling price of $13.95 limits average revenue per order to $13.16 — the lowest of any cuisine. Hamburger and Cheeseburger together account for $16,348 (57% of American revenue). Hot Dog and Veggie Burger fall below the Review threshold and should be removed.

Weekday lunch 12:00: $14,832 ~ 2.1× the weekend equivalent ($7,042).
However, weekend lunch generates $54.24 revenue per table per day vs weekday's $44.42, as more orders are placed per table on weekends --- not because individual customers spend more.

Asian fills seats and turns tables; Italian maximises how much each customer spends.

Why February Leads on Average Daily Revenue: February's $1,832 average daily revenue exceeds January ($1,754) and March ($1,778) despite its total revenue being the lowest of the three months. The driver is not a spike or special event — it is a shorter month (28 days) combined with a concentration of strong weekday evenings. February dinner revenue ($23,893) closely tracks January ($24,737) and March ($24,490), but the daily average is higher simply because there are fewer days to divide by. February 1 was the single highest-revenue day of the entire quarter at $2,425.
Holiday Impact: Presidents Day (20 February) generated $2,166 — the seventh-highest single day in Q1 and 19% above the February regular-day average. January's three holiday days averaged $2,045 — also above the January regular average. Holidays do not reduce revenue; guests who choose to dine out on a public holiday tend to spend above the regular-day average. March had no public holidays. The data supports keeping the restaurant open on holidays.
Lunch vs Dinner in February: February dinner revenue ($23,893) exceeded lunch ($20,165) by $3,728 — the widest dinner-lunch gap of any month. This reinforces the finding that dinner is the primary revenue driver, and that February's stronger daily average is concentrated in the evening. There is no evidence of table occupancy or time-at-table variation in the dataset.

Wednesday 16:00 Anomaly: Wednesday already has early-evening appetite — 80 orders and $2,484 at 16:00, the highest 16:00 of any day. Customers arrive early but do not stay into later dinner slots. The 19:00 drop-off is the problem. The most targeted fix is a structured offer for the 18:00–21:00 window — not a generic Wednesday promotion. A fixed-price dinner or group booking incentive focused on that specific window would address the gap hour-by-hour.

Holiday Operating Recommendation: On major public holidays, operate a dinner-only service (17:00–22:00). This reduces staffing costs, focuses service hours on the highest-earning meal period, and avoids quieter early-hour trading that brings down holiday averages. Do not close on holidays — both total revenue and per-table dinner earnings are above the regular-day average. Holiday dinner revenue per table ($67.74) is 21% above weekday dinner ($55.98) and 37% above weekend dinner ($49.45).
Table Distribution: Revenue across all 15 tables ranges from $10,146.50 (T15) to $11,851.50 (T14) — a spread of only $1,705 (17%). Table position is not a meaningful revenue lever. Staffing and seating decisions should focus on meal period and day type, not table location.
