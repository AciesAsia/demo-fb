KPI Overview — Q1 2023
📊 Key Insights
  • The restaurant generated $160,783 in total revenue across Q1 2023 (90 days), averaging $1,786/day and $29.96 per order.
  • With 5,366 orders and 12,218 items sold, customers ordered an average of 2.28 items per visit — indicating strong upsell and combo performance.
  • Italian cuisine led revenue at 31% share ($49,852), followed by Asian at 29.3% — together accounting for over 60% of total revenue.
  • March was the strongest month at $55,997, while February — despite the fewest days — delivered the highest average daily revenue at $1,832/day.

Daily & Hourly Revenue — Q1 2023
📊 Key Insights
  • Lunch dominates revenue at 58% ($93,254) vs dinner at 42% ($67,529), with twin peaks at 12:00 ($21,874) and 19:00 ($21,120).
  • The mid-afternoon trough (15:00–16:00, ~$7,280 combined) represents an underutilised window — a targeted afternoon promotion could recover incremental revenue.
  • Evening trade (18:00–21:00) accounts for approximately $72,720 (45%) of daily revenue, making dinner service operationally critical despite lower volume.
  • Revenue drops sharply after 22:00 ($1,476) — late-night trade contributes less than 1% of daily revenue and may not justify full staffing.

Menu Intelligence — Q1 2023
📊 Key Insights
  • Stars (high orders, high revenue) are led by Korean Beef Bowl ($13,022), Spaghetti & Meatballs ($12,438), and Hamburger ($9,557) — these 11 items are the revenue engine.
  • Cash Cows (high revenue, lower volume) like Pork Ramen and Meat Lasagna generate strong per-item value and should be protected on the menu despite modest order counts.
  • Workhorses — French Fries, Mac & Cheese, Chips & Salsa — are high-volume at low price points ($5–$7); they drive traffic and upsell opportunities but thin margins.
  • Review/Remove candidates (14 items) include Shrimp Scampi, Cheese Lasagna, and Hot Dog — low orders and low revenue; consolidating this list could reduce kitchen complexity and food waste.

Trends — Q1 2023
📊 Key Insights
  • Weekly revenue held in a narrow $11,870–$12,740 band across all 13 weeks, indicating stable and predictable demand without significant volatility.
  • Week 11 ($12,740) was the peak week and Week 5 ($11,870) the trough — a difference of just 7%, pointing to a highly consistent operation.

  • The flat weekly trend suggests revenue growth will require deliberate intervention — new menu items, promotional events, or expanded capacity — rather than organic lift.

February 2023 Deep-Dive
📊 Key Insights
  • Despite having the fewest days (28), February achieved the highest average daily revenue at $1,832 — outperforming January ($1,725) and March ($1,806).
  • February lunch revenue ($29,520) and dinner revenue ($21,776) both tracked closely to Q1 averages, suggesting no single daypart drove the outperformance — it was broad-based.
  • February hourly revenue exceeded the Q1 average at key peak hours (12:00: $22,400 vs $21,874; 19:00: $21,800 vs $21,120), amplifying the daily revenue advantage.
  • The February premium is likely driven by Valentine's Day (14 Feb) dining demand — a high-value event to plan proactively with set menus and reservation management in Q1 2024.

Day Segments — Q1 2023
📊 Key Insights
  • Monday is the strongest day at $2,017 avg daily revenue — 31% above Wednesday ($1,540), which is the weakest day of the week.
  • The mid-week trough (Wed–Thu) is a clear structural pattern: Wednesday ($1,540) and Thursday ($1,690) consistently underperform. Targeted mid-week promotions or events could close the gap.
  • Weekend performance is solid — Saturday ($1,960) and Sunday ($1,880) rank 2nd and 3rd — but neither surpasses Monday, suggesting weekday loyal regulars are the primary revenue driver.
  • Friday ($1,850) shows a modest pre-weekend lift but does not spike as sharply as might be expected, indicating the restaurant's customer base skews toward regular weekday diners over weekend occasion visitors.

Key Finding
